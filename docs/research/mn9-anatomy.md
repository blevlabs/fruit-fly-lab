# Adult MN9 → muscle 9: anatomical evidence and attachment geometry

Historical literature and geometry review: 2026-09-11. Scope: adult *Drosophila melanogaster*. The anatomical findings and proposed representation below precede the current package; they do not report new simulation results.

For current package evidence, see [status](../status.md); planned work is in the [roadmap](../roadmap.md). Historical artifacts are indexed in the [results provenance archive](../../results/README.md).

**MN9 innervates ipsilateral muscle 9, which pulls through a tendon on the free dorsal arm of the rostrum’s internal apodeme to protract the rostrum.** The supported attachment is **ventral head capsule → tendon → rostral apodeme**, not head → labellum. The published Shiu supplementary tables explicitly identify `720575940660219265` as `MN9_r`. Anatomical topology is supported; metric muscle attachments, moment arms, and muscle constitutive parameters remain unmeasured in the material recovered here. [McKellar et al., 2020, Results and Fig. 7](https://elifesciences.org/articles/54978#fig7), [Schwarz et al., 2017, Fig. 3C and Discussion](https://elifesciences.org/articles/19892#fig3), [Shiu et al., 2024, supplementary workbook](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41586-024-07763-9/MediaObjects/41586_2024_7763_MOESM2_ESM.xlsx).

## Minimal defensible attachment recommendation

Start with a **unilateral, sagittal m9–rostrum mechanics assay**. It can test transmission and force production without claiming complete proboscis behavior. Use the existing head–rostrum pivot provisionally. Represent the rostral apodeme as a rigid attachment frame on the rostrum, with no additional apodeme joint unless future geometry demonstrates one is needed.

| Landmark | Owner / frame | Placement rule | Numeric status |
|---|---|---|---|
| J: rostrum pivot | Head | Existing rig origin above, subsequently registered to the anterior-head articulation in anatomical images | Model-derived, provisional |
| O9: origin centroid | Head | Ipsilateral ventral head-wall attachment patch | Unknown; do not substitute J or an arbitrary exterior mesh vertex |
| I9: tendon insertion | Rostrum/apodeme | Ipsilateral free dorsal arm of the rostral apodeme, offset from J | Unknown; a zero-offset insertion would produce zero hinge moment |
| Contralateral O9/I9 | Head and rostrum | Mirror only as an explicitly estimated geometric symmetry assumption | Estimated geometry does not establish bilateral neural drive |

Use one tension path from O9 to I9 as the initial approximation to the distributed muscle and tendon. If images establish tendon routing around a structure, include that routing; no pulley or wrapping path is established here. Lumping the origin footprint into one centroid and the tendon into a straight path is an explicit approximation, not measured fiber architecture.

For head-frame origin **O** and rostrum-local insertion **b**, derive the moving endpoint **B(q) = J + R(q)b**, path length **ℓ(q) = ‖B(q) − O‖**, and path velocity **ℓ̇ = (∂ℓ/∂q)q̇**. Apply the muscle/tendon tension along **(O − B)/ℓ**; the corresponding generalized torque is **τ = −F ∂ℓ/∂q**. Thus the moment arm changes with attachment geometry. The activation/length/velocity/passive-force model supplies **F**; MN9 does not supply a target angle. If tendon compliance is included, distinguish total path length from contractile-fiber length.

Before choosing numeric O9/I9, digitize the pivot, head origin patch, tendon endpoint, and rostrum–haustellum landmark in a scale-bearing sagittal image, recording the image/panel, pixels, µm/pixel, pose, and landmark uncertainty. Register those points to the rig using anatomical landmarks and document the residual. A single section supplies projected x/z estimates only; lateral depth requires the source stack or a separately justified symmetry estimate. **No numerical attachment pair is recommended yet:** inventing millimeter offsets would falsely upgrade anatomical topology into measured geometry.

Geometric acceptance checks should verify nonzero m9 moment arm with the protraction sign in the registered pose, agreement between length derivative and applied torque, and no unexplained cuticle crossings. Select geometry from images before comparing movement; do not move attachments or increase force to reproduce a desired extension angle. Use independent anatomy/force data and hold-out kinematics to constrain the result. The 2024 model explicitly states that the MN9 firing-rate/rostrum-lifting relationship is unknown. [Shiu et al., 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07763-9).

A rostrum-only assay may keep the distal assembly rigid as a declared reduction; it cannot reproduce independent haustellum motion. Full feeding needs additional joints and separately innervated muscles. Any passive restoring force is an estimated mechanical term until measured; it must not stand in for an inferred antagonist spike train or enforce a programmed return stroke.

## Sensory and quantitative gaps that remain

Adult **sd-L neurons** at the labellum–haustellum junction are a relevant movement-feedback candidate. Yu et al. identify Iav-expressing sd-L neurons responding to labellum displacement and report GRASP signals with MN4-, MN6-, and MN9-labeled populations. This supports investigating distal proprioceptive feedback, not treating rostrum angle as the measured sensory input. Their MN9 experiment uses R18B07; that driver is not MN9-exclusive in the 2020 survey. Therefore GRASP does not establish a uniquely resolved sd-L → this exact FlyWire MN9 connection. No sd-L root assignment or angle-to-spike calibration was established here. [Yu et al., 2023, Figs. 2 and 5](https://journals.plos.org/plosgenetics/article?id=10.1371/journal.pgen.1010562), [McKellar Table 2](https://elifesciences.org/articles/54978#table2).

Adult NOMPC-dependent labellar bristle and peg mechanosensory neurons provide another identified route for physical contact feedback: bristle stimulation promotes spreading, whereas peg stimulation promotes retraction. These are contact sensors, not a measured m9 stretch receptor or a rule to retract on hot food. Their relevant physical inputs are local sensillum deflections. [Zhou et al., 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6531006/).

Remaining inputs required for the mechanistic chain: **m9 origin/insertion coordinates and footprint; fiber length, pennation and cross-sectional area; tendon slack length/compliance; adult m9 NMJ transmission and activation kinetics; force–length/velocity/passive-force parameters; passive joint torque; contralateral and antagonist identities in the modeled graph; and identified sensory roots with measured mechanical response curves.** None can be recovered from a neuron root ID or external mesh alone. This report establishes an anatomical starting point; it does not establish a calibrated or closed-loop biological proboscis model.
